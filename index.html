Stage 01 // Point of Entry

The Lips & Oral Cavity

Combustion gases reach temperatures up to 900°C at the cigarette tip. Upon contact, heat and concentrated toxins immediately alter oral mucosal tissue.

Capillary Constriction

Nicotine reduces blood supply to periodontal tissues, leading to chronic gum degradation and tooth loss.

DNA Alkylation

Carcinogens like benzene and N-nitrosamines permanently damage cellular DNA, triggering oral cancer pathways.

Stage 02 // The Conduit

Throat & Trachea

As smoke descends into the trachea, chemical irritants paralyze the respiratory tract's primary cleaning mechanism in seconds.

Ciliary Stasis

Microscopic cilia designed to sweep out debris are frozen by formaldehyde, trapping toxic particulate inside.

Laryngeal Edema

Vocal cords suffer chronic inflammation, causing voice deepening, hoarseness, and elevated dysplasia risk.

Stage 03 // Respiratory Breakdown

Lungs & Alveoli

500 million fragile air sacs are flooded with sticky particulate. Over time, elasticity is destroyed and lung volume shrinks.

Emphysematous Rupture

Alveolar walls collapse into enlarged, inefficient spaces, leaving sufferers permanently short of breath.

Tar Condensation

A pack-a-day smoker deposits approximately 150ml (a full cup) of viscous black tar into lung tissues annually.

Stage 04 // Systemic Distribution

Bloodstream & Heart

Nicotine crosses the blood-brain barrier in 7 seconds. Meanwhile, carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin, starving vital organs of oxygen.

Accelerated Atherosclerosis

Oxidative stress damages arterial walls, causing rapid plaque buildup and dramatically escalating stroke risk.

Compensatory Tachycardia

With oxygen capacity reduced by carbon monoxide, the heart is forced to beat faster against hardened blood vessels.
