Stage 01 / Point of Entry

The Lips & Oral Cavity

Every puff exposes the lips, gums, and tongue to toxic combustion gases reaching temperatures up to 900°C.

Gum Recession & Tooth Loss

Nicotine restricts blood flow to capillaries in the mouth, causing chronic gum disease and dead tissue.

Cellular Mutation

Formaldehyde and benzene instantly bind to DNA in oral mucosal cells, drastically elevating oral cancer risk.

Stage 02 / The Pathway

The Throat & Vocal Cords

As smoke moves down the trachea, chemical irritants paralyze the body's natural defense systems within seconds.

Cilia Paralysis

Microscopic hair-like structures (cilia) meant to sweep out dust and microbes are completely immobilized by toxic tar.

Vocal Cord Inflammation

Hot toxic gases inflame larynx tissue, causing chronic hoarseness and structural vocal changes.

Stage 03 / Deep Impact

The Lungs & Alveoli

Over 500 million tiny air sacs (alveoli) are flooded with carbon monoxide, heavy metals, and black tar residue.

Alveolar Destruction (Emphysema)

Fragile air sac walls rupture permanently, decreasing lung surface area and making breathing feel like suffocating.

Tar Accumulation

A pack-a-day smoker inhales a full cup of pure sticky tar into their lungs every single year.

Stage 04 / Systemic Damage

Bloodstream & Heart

Nicotine reaches the brain in 7 seconds. Carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin, starving every organ in the body of oxygen.

Arterial Stiffening & Plaque

Toxins tear the inner lining of blood vessels, triggering rapid cholesterol buildup, stroke, and heart attack risks.

Chronic Hypoxia

Carbon monoxide replaces vital oxygen in blood cells, forcing the heart to work twice as hard to maintain survival.
