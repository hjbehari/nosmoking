Chapters 02 & 03

Gas Exchange vs. Smoke Inhalation

Under healthy conditions, 300 million alveoli transfer oxygen into capillaries while removing carbon dioxide. When cigarette aerosol is introduced, tar and micro-particulates disrupt this delicate membrane.

Physiological Mode: Oxygenation

Blue particles ($O_2$) travel through the bronchial tree down to alveoli capillaries, exchanging with returning red particles ($CO_2$).

Chapter 06

Pathological Disease Profiles

Review clinical mechanisms, cellular pathology, and diagnostic indicators for major smoking-induced pulmonary disorders.

COPD

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

Progressive airflow limitation caused by a combination of small airway disease (obstructive bronchiolitis) and parenchymal destruction (emphysema).

Key Indicator: Irreversible FEV1 decline & chronic dyspnea.
EMP

Pulmonary Emphysema

Permanent enlargement of air spaces distal to terminal bronchioles accompanied by destruction of alveolar walls without obvious fibrosis.

Key Indicator: Loss of alveolar capillary bed surface area.
CBR

Chronic Bronchitis

Hypersecretion of bronchial mucus resulting in productive cough for at least 3 consecutive months in 2 consecutive years.

Key Indicator: Goblet cell hyperplasia & submucosal edema.
Chapter 07

Physiological Recovery Following Cessation

The body begins repairing damage immediately following nicotine withdrawal. Explore physiological repair milestones verified by clinical studies.

20 MINUTES

Cardiovascular Stabilization

Heart rate and peripheral blood pressure drop back toward baseline levels. Peripheral circulation begins to normalize.

12 HOURS

Carbon Monoxide Normalization

Bloodstream carbon monoxide ($CO$) levels fall to normal range, increasing arterial blood oxygen capacity.

1-9 MONTHS

Ciliary Regeneration & Mucociliary Clearance

Cilia regrow in bronchial pathways, increasing the capacity to clear mucus, handle pulmonary secretions, and reduce infection risk.

10 YEARS

Lung Cancer Risk Reduction

Lung cancer death risk drops to roughly half that of a continuing smoker. Risk of mouth, throat, esophagus, and bladder cancer decreases significantly.
